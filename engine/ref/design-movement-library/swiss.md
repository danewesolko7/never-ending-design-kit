## 2. Swiss and International Typographic Style (1940s to 1980s)
- **Concept:** total objectivity. The designer is an invisible go-between who presents information with mathematical clarity.
- **Feel:** institutional, highly organised, corporate, clean, timeless, authoritative.
- **Layout:** everything aligns to a strict multi-column modular grid. Use large, asymmetric white space. Hierarchy must be so clear that size contrast alone tells the reader the reading order.
- **Texture:** crisp, high-contrast, clinical surfaces. Photographs are objective, documentary black and white, never stylised or filtered.
- **Type:** classic, highly legible sans-serifs (Helvetica, Univers, Akzidenz-Grotesk, or a close modern grotesk). Tight tracking for headings, normal for body. Build hierarchy from weight (light, regular, bold) within one family, never by mixing families.
- **Tone and copy:** informational, neutral, very clear, logical, understated. No emotional exaggeration or sales talk.
- **Touchstones:** Josef Muller-Brockmann, Armin Hofmann, Emil Ruder, Jacqueline Casey. Also Karl Gerstner and Wim Crouwel for grid systems.
- **Prompt keywords:** International Typographic Style, mathematical grid layout, corporate Swiss design, high-contrast black and white photography, flush-left ragged-right typography.
- **Rule:** total objectivity. The design is an invisible container for the message.
