## 2. Typography as layout
Moving the same heading and block left or right is positioning, not typography. Each page gets its own typographic idea; these are the tools:
- **Weight contrast inside a line** (Swiss: weight, not new families), where a heading has a subject and a predicate, not on every heading.
- **Lead-ins that break the column**, hung outside the text block.
- **Stacked or stepped headings**, with lines offset on the grid.
- **A diagonal plane** the heading straddles (Bauhaus vector), with contrast holding on both sides.
- **Rotated type** for one-word labels that run up a margin (horizontal on phones).
- **Text wrapped round a shape** (`shape-outside`).
- **Scale contrast:** one very large element per view against quiet body text.
- **Vary what leads** a section: a heading, a lead-in, a figure or an image.
- **Reading rules hold (AAA 1.4.8):** body text flush left, never justified, lines under 80 characters, line spacing 1.5. Experiments are for headings, lead-ins and labels.
