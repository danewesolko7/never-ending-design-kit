## 1. Copy tells (S5, S8)
Copy reads as machine-written when a word fits any business. tools/tells.py (run by pass.py) fails the hard tells and warns on the soft ones past a cap; the lists are distilled from Taste Skill's copy tells (MIT, credited in NOTICE).
- Hard: stand-in words (seamless, unlock, elevate, robust, bespoke, world-class ...), stock lines ("in today's fast-paced world", "look no further"), chat or template residue, the page talking about itself ("this section shows").
- Soft (capped): em dashes, exclamation marks, "not X but Y" frames and their substitutes (rather than, instead of), "no X, no Y" runs, virtue words about ourselves (honest, genuinely, thoughtful), two-fragment headings ("Twelve rooms. Three ways."), round proof figures, the default cast of invented names.
- The fix is a fact from the brief in the word's place, never a synonym. Over-correcting breeds new tells: calm vocabulary instead of hype, everything in pairs instead of threes, a quip closing every paragraph. End on information; let length follow the material.
- A word literal for the trade (a climbing harness, bespoke tailoring) goes in brief.json copy_allow.
- Eyebrow labels over every heading, a strip of figures under the hero and three polished testimonials are section templates, not copy: the content-form map replaces them.
